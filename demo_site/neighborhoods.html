Five neighborhoods we know by heart.

Montréal is the largest city in Québec, built on an island in the St. Lawrence River around Mount Royal. French is the official language of Québec, and English is also widely spoken in the city. Here are the five areas where we work most often.

Indicative information only. Descriptions are general, and the price ranges are fictional estimates for this demo site. Visit an area at different times of day, and in different seasons if you can, before deciding.

Le Plateau-Mont-Royal

A lively, central neighborhood known for its colourful row houses with outdoor staircases, its cafés, shops and restaurants, and its proximity to Mount Royal Park. It is served by the orange metro line and has many bike paths.

  • Best for: young professionals, families who like city life, plex investors.
  • Property types: duplexes and triplexes, apartments in plexes, some condos.
  • Indicative prices (fictional): 4½ from $1,900 per month; triplexes from $1,200,000.
  • Things to check: street parking permits, insulation and windows in older buildings.
  • Our listings here: LI-004, LI-007.

Griffintown

A former industrial area next to downtown and the Lachine Canal, transformed over the last fifteen years with many new condo buildings, restaurants and shops. The canal path is popular for walking and cycling.

  • Best for: professionals working downtown, newcomers, buyers of new condos.
  • Property types: recent condos, furnished apartments, some lofts.
  • Indicative prices (fictional): 1-bedroom condos from $450,000; furnished 4½ from $2,400 per month.
  • Things to check: condo fees, the contingency fund, and construction projects nearby.
  • Our listings here: LI-002.

Verdun

A residential borough along the St. Lawrence River, with a long riverside park, an urban beach and Wellington Street, a busy commercial street with shops and restaurants. It is served by the green metro line.

  • Best for: families, first-time buyers, people who enjoy being near the water.
  • Property types: townhouses, duplexes, apartments, some new condos.
  • Indicative prices (fictional): townhouses from $700,000; 4½ from $1,700 per month.
  • Things to check: whether the property is in a flood zone, and the condition of the basement.
  • Our listings here: LI-003.

Notre-Dame-de-Grâce (NDG)

A large residential neighborhood in the west of the city, with tree-lined streets, parks and local shops. It has a long bilingual history, with both French-speaking and English-speaking communities, and is popular with families. It is served by the orange metro line.

  • Best for: families, students, English-speaking newcomers.
  • Property types: semi-detached cottages, duplexes, apartments in small buildings.
  • Indicative prices (fictional): cottages from $1,000,000; 3½ from $1,400 per month.
  • Things to check: distance to the metro, as some streets are only served by buses.
  • Our listings here: LI-001, LI-006.

Old Montréal (Vieux-Montréal)

The historic heart of the city, next to the Old Port, with cobblestone streets and stone buildings from the 18th and 19th centuries. Many of these buildings now house condos, offices, hotels and restaurants. It is busy with visitors in summer.

  • Best for: buyers of character condos, companies looking for prestige offices.
  • Property types: condos in converted buildings, lofts, offices.
  • Indicative prices (fictional): condos from $500,000; offices from $27 per sq ft per year.
  • Things to check: noise from events and terraces in summer, and parking costs.
  • Our listings here: LI-005, LI-008.
