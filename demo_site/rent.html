Renting a home or an office in Montréal.

Furnished or unfurnished, for a few months or several years: how renting works in Montréal, what you pay before moving in, and which documents to prepare.

General information, not legal advice. Residential leases in Québec are governed by the Civil Code of Québec, and disputes are handled by the Tribunal administratif du logement (TAL). Rules can change; check the TAL's official information or a lawyer for your situation.

Understanding apartment sizes: 3½, 4½, 5½

In Québec, apartments are described by their number of rooms. The kitchen, living room and bedrooms each count as one room, and the bathroom counts as a "half".

Québec apartment sizes
NameWhat it means
1½ or 2½Studio: one main room (sometimes with a separate kitchen) and a bathroom
3½1 bedroom, living room, kitchen, bathroom
4½2 bedrooms, living room, kitchen, bathroom
5½3 bedrooms, living room, kitchen, bathroom

The renting process

  1. Tell us what you needNeighborhood, monthly budget, size (3½, 4½…), furnished or not, whether heating and electricity should be included, pets, and your move-in date.
  2. VisitsWe organize visits of matching apartments, usually within 48 hours. Visits are free for tenants.
  3. Rental applicationYou fill in an application and, with your written consent, the landlord may check your credit and references. Landlords usually answer within 2 to 5 working days.
  4. Lease signingIn Québec, residential leases must be written on the mandatory lease form. It states the rent, the duration, what is included (heating, hot water, appliances, parking) and the rules of the building. Each party keeps a copy.
  5. First month's rentYou pay the first month's rent, usually on or before the first day of the lease.
  6. Move-in inventory and keysWe do a detailed inventory with photos, room by room, signed by you and the landlord. Then you receive the keys.

Example: for an unfurnished 4½ at $2,100 per month, you pay $2,100 (the first month's rent) before moving in, and nothing else to our agency.

  • After the first month, rent is paid monthly, on the first day of each month, by bank transfer, Interac e-Transfer or cheque. A landlord generally cannot require post-dated cheques.
  • Electricity is often paid by the tenant directly to the electricity provider. Check on the lease whether heating and hot water are included.
  • Tenant insurance is not always mandatory, but it is strongly recommended, and many landlords ask for it in the lease.

Documents required for tenants

Individuals

  • Valid photo ID (passport or driver's licence).
  • Proof of income: recent pay stubs, an employment letter, or bank statements for self-employed people.
  • Written consent for a credit check (newcomers without Canadian credit history can provide other proof of solvency).
  • References from a previous landlord, if available.
  • Students and newcomers: study or work permit and letter of acceptance or job offer. Some landlords may ask for a guarantor (endorser).

Companies (office or staff housing)

  • Company registration details (Québec enterprise number).
  • ID of the company representative signing the lease.
  • Recent financial statements for commercial leases.

July 1st: Moving Day

Many Montréal leases run from July 1st to June 30th, so a very large number of people move on the same day. If you want to move around July 1st, start looking in the spring and book movers early. We also have apartments available at other times of the year.

Renewal and leaving

In Québec, a residential lease is generally renewed automatically at the end of its term, and the tenant has the right to stay in the apartment. For a lease of 12 months or more, a tenant who does not want to renew usually has to send written notice to the landlord 3 to 6 months before the end of the lease, and a landlord who wants to change the rent or conditions must send notice in the same period. Disagreements about rent increases can be brought to the TAL.
