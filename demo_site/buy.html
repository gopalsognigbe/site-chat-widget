Buying in Montréal, from pre-approval to keys.

From your mortgage pre-approval to the day you receive your keys: how buying a condo, plex or house works with Fleuve Immobilier in Montréal.

General information, not legal advice. Property laws, taxes and procedures in Québec and Canada change over time and depend on your situation. Always confirm the details of your purchase with a notary, a lawyer or your lender.

How buying works, step by step

  1. Define your project and get pre-approvedWe meet (in person or by video call) to discuss the type of property, neighborhoods, budget and timing. We recommend getting a mortgage pre-approval from your bank or a mortgage broker before visiting, so you know your real budget. This first meeting is free.
  2. Property search and visitsWe send you a shortlist of properties that match your criteria and organize visits, usually grouped on the same day. If you are not yet in Montréal, we can do live video visits.
  3. Promise to purchaseWhen you choose a property, we help you write a promise to purchase (promesse d'achat): the price, the deposit, the occupancy date and your conditions, usually financing, inspection and review of documents. The seller can accept, refuse or make a counter-offer.
  4. Pre-purchase inspectionWithin the delay set in the promise (often 7 to 10 days), a building inspector examines the property and gives you a written report. If serious problems are found, you can renegotiate or withdraw as allowed by your promise.
  5. Financing and document reviewYour lender confirms the mortgage. You review the seller's declarations, the certificate of location and, for a condo, the syndicate documents (declaration of co-ownership, minutes of meetings, contingency fund, condo fees).
  6. Signing the deed at the notaryYou choose a notary, who prepares the deed of sale and, if you have a mortgage, the hypothec deed. Buyer and seller sign in front of the notary, and the funds are transferred through the notary's trust account.
  7. Registration and welcome taxThe notary registers the sale in the Land Register of Québec. A few weeks to a few months later, the city sends you the bill for the transfer duties, commonly called the "welcome tax".
  8. Keys and occupancyYou receive the keys on the occupancy date set in the promise to purchase, often the day of the signing or a few days later.

Documents needed to buy

The exact list is set by your lender and your notary, but buyers are usually asked for:

  • Two pieces of valid ID (for example a passport and a driver's licence).
  • Your mortgage pre-approval, then the final mortgage commitment from your lender.
  • Proof of your down payment and of the origin of the funds (bank statements, gift letter if a family member helps).
  • Proof of your marital status and matrimonial regime, which the notary needs to draft the deed.
  • For newcomers: proof of your immigration status in Canada (permanent resident card or permit), as federal rules on foreign buyers may apply (see the FAQ).
  • Proof of home insurance, which lenders require before the signing.

From the seller, the notary will require their title, a recent certificate of location, the seller's declarations, municipal and school tax bills and, for a condo, the syndicate documents.

The role of the notary

In Québec, notaries are legal professionals who play a central role in real estate. The notary is impartial: they act for the transaction, not for the agency. Their role is to:

  • check the seller's title and any rights, servitudes or hypothecs in the Land Register of Québec;
  • review the certificate of location and the other documents of the property;
  • draft the deed of sale and, if needed, the hypothec (mortgage) deed;
  • hold the funds in trust and pay the seller, the seller's lender and the agency;
  • calculate the adjustments of municipal and school taxes and condo fees between buyer and seller;
  • register the sale and keep the original deed, giving you certified copies.

The buyer usually chooses the notary and pays the notary's fees. If you do not have one, we can suggest several notaries in Montréal who work in English and French. See our fees page for what is and is not included in prices.

Our advice before buying

  • Always make your promise to purchase conditional on an inspection, even for a new condo.
  • For a condo, check the contingency fund and any special assessments planned by the syndicate.
  • Ask how the property is heated and how much heating cost last winter.
  • Check the basement for signs of water infiltration, and ask whether the property is in a flood zone.
  • Budget for the welcome tax, notary fees, inspection and moving costs on top of the price.
